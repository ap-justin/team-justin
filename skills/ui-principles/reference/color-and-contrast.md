# Color & contrast

Whether the palette is a system or a pile of literals, and whether the values in it are legible. Contrast is the one visual property that is genuinely computable from source — when both foreground and background resolve to values in the token file, the ratio is arithmetic, not judgement.

---

## Every color is a step on a scale, not a literal

**Principle:** components reference tokens. Raw hex, `rgb()`, and `hsl()` literals belong in the token file and nowhere else.
**Mechanism:** a literal in a component is a value with no relationships — nothing tells the next reader which shade it sits between, so the next shade gets eyeballed against it and the scale silently forks. Scales exist so a component never has to derive a color.
**Code signal:**
  - hex/`rgb()`/`hsl()` literals in `.tsx`/`.svelte`/component CSS — `color: #3b82f6`, `bg-[#f7f7f7]`
  - two near-identical literals in different files (`#f8fafc` and `#f9fafb`) that were meant to be the same surface
  - a token file with fewer steps than the UI needs, so components fill the gaps themselves
**Fix:** move the value into the scale as its own step and reference the token; if it duplicates an existing step, delete it.
**Detect:** STATIC
**Source:** *Refactoring UI* (Wathan & Schoger, 2018) · https://www.refactoringui.com/previews/building-your-color-palette (2018)

---

## Neither end of a scale is pure

**Principle:** the darkest step is a very dark gray, the lightest a very light one. Pure `#000` and pure `#fff` are not steps.
**Mechanism:** nothing in the physical world is pure black, so pure black surfaces read as a hole rather than as depth, and pure white against it maximizes halation — the glow that makes text edges smear, worst for astigmatic readers and in dark themes.
**Code signal:**
  - `#000` / `black` / `rgb(0,0,0)` as a text color or surface, `#fff` / `white` as a page background in a dark theme's inverse
  - a token scale whose endpoints are `0%` and `100%` lightness
  - `text-black` / `bg-black` used as the default body color in a component
**Fix:** pull both endpoints in — a near-black around 8–12% lightness, a near-white a step off pure — and reference them as tokens.
**Applies when:** OLED-targeted "true black" themes are a deliberate power trade; a stated one is answered, an accidental `#000` is not.
**Detect:** STATIC
**Source:** *Refactoring UI* (Wathan & Schoger, 2018) — verified at https://www.refactoringui.com/previews/building-your-color-palette (2018) · https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/ (2020)

## Saturation rises as lightness leaves the middle

**Principle:** the light and dark ends of a ramp carry *more* saturation than its mid step, not the same value.
**Mechanism:** in HSL the saturation component loses force as lightness approaches 0% or 100% — the same S at 90% lightness reads far less colorful than at 50% — so a ramp built by holding S and moving L washes out at both ends, and the tints stop reading as the brand hue on exactly the large surfaces they get used for.
**Code signal:**
  - a token ramp whose every step shares one `S` value — `hsl(220 60% 95%)` … `hsl(220 60% 20%)`
  - steps generated by a function that only moves lightness: `lighten($blue, 40%)`, `darken()`, `color-mix(in srgb, var(--blue), white 80%)`
  - a `--brand-50` / `--brand-900` pair sitting visibly grayer than the mid steps beside them
**Fix:** hand-tune S upward at both ends — and where S is already maxed, rotate the hue toward a brighter neighbour (yellow/cyan/magenta read lighter than red/green/blue at equal lightness) — or author the ramp in OKLCH.
**Applies when:** ramps authored in OKLCH/LCH answer this by construction; the entry is about ramps built by moving HSL lightness alone.
**Detect:** STATIC
**Source:** *Refactoring UI* (Wathan & Schoger, 2018), pp. 152–157 — incl. perceived brightness by hue

## Neutrals carry a hue

**Principle:** grays are built with a small amount of saturation, tilted toward the palette's own hue — cool with a blue-leaning brand, warm with a red/yellow-leaning one.
**Mechanism:** a zero-saturation gray belongs to no palette, so it reads as unstyled next to any saturated color. The tint is what makes the neutrals look chosen rather than defaulted, at a saturation low enough that nobody can name the color.
**Code signal:**
  - a gray ramp at exactly `hsl(x 0% y%)` / equal RGB channels (`#888888`, `#cccccc`) across every step
  - two gray ramps in one system with different hues (a cool border gray against warm text gray) — tinted, but not tinted *together*
  - the framework's default gray used beside a custom brand ramp
**Fix:** rebuild the neutral ramp at a low saturation on the brand hue, one hue for the whole ramp.
**Detect:** STATIC
**Source:** *Refactoring UI* (Wathan & Schoger, 2018) · https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/composing-a-palette (2022) — the "natural pairing" of a hue-saturated gray to the accent

## Opacity is translucency, not a shade

**Principle:** alpha is for genuine see-through — glass over content, a scrim over imagery, a disabled state. A lighter or darker version of a color is its own solid step.
**Mechanism:** an alpha value renders differently over every background it lands on, so a "10% accent" surface is a different color in each context and none of them are in the scale. It also can't be contrast-checked, because its rendered value depends on what's behind it.
**Code signal:**
  - `bg-accent/10`, `text-gray-900/60`, `border-white/20` on solid UI
  - `rgba(brand, .08)` / `hsl(… / .15)` as a surface or text fill
  - `opacity-50` on a whole element to mute its text, or `color-mix(in srgb, var(--brand), white 40%)` to fake a tint
**Fix:** add the solid step to the scale and use it; keep alpha for scrims, glass, and disabled.
**Applies when:** this is also `/taste-review`'s hard rule — report it once, in whichever pass is running, not twice.
**Detect:** STATIC
**Source:** *Refactoring UI* (Wathan & Schoger, 2018) · https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html (2023)

## Muted text on a colored surface is a tint of that surface

**Principle:** on a colored background, de-emphasized text is a hand-picked step sharing the background's hue — never a step from the neutral ramp, never white at reduced alpha.
**Mechanism:** grey-on-white de-emphasizes because it *reduces contrast*, not because it is grey. Moved onto a saturated field the same grey no longer sits between the background and the ink, so it reads as a foreign color rather than a quieter one. White at reduced alpha does lower the contrast, but it desaturates toward the background — dull, washed out, often read as disabled — and over imagery the background shows through the glyphs themselves.
**Code signal:**
  - `text-gray-400` / `text-slate-300` / `color: #9ca3af` on a child of an element whose background is a brand or accent token
  - `text-white/70`, `rgba(255,255,255,.7)` as label or body text on a colored, gradient, or photographic surface
  - a neutral-ramp secondary-text token used inside a hero, banner, or filled card
**Fix:** add a step on the background's own hue — same hue, lightness and saturation tuned until the contrast drops — and reference it; keep alpha for scrims.
**Applies when:** the tint still owes the WCAG floor — de-emphasis moves toward the background, not below 4.5:1. Cross-ref "Opacity is translucency, not a shade" for why the alpha shortcut isn't a step.
**Detect:** STATIC
**Source:** *Refactoring UI* (Wathan & Schoger, 2018), pp. 42–45 · https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html (2023)

## Text contrast is computed, not eyeballed

**Principle:** every foreground/background pair that both resolve in source is checked against 4.5:1 (body) and 3:1 (≥18.66px bold or ≥24px).
**Mechanism:** perceived contrast is not lightness difference — two steps that look separated in a token file's swatch row can fail the ratio, and mid-tone pairs fail most often. The formula is the only reliable read, and it runs on values you already have.
**Code signal:**
  - a muted text token on a light surface at the light end of the ramp — `text-gray-400` on `bg-white`, placeholder tokens, disabled labels used for real content
  - text set on a *colored* surface (accent, success, warning) where the text token was chosen for the neutral surface
  - a dark theme reusing the light theme's text token against a dark surface
**Fix:** move the text one or two steps darker/lighter until the pair clears; if the surface is the problem, change the surface.
**Applies when:** the tier drops to HEURISTIC when the background is inherited from an ancestor you can't resolve, or is an image — say which, and hand the image case to `/visual-review`.
**Detect:** STATIC
**Source:** https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html (2023) · https://webaim.org/articles/contrast/ (WebAIM, rev. ongoing)

## Borders, icons and focus rings need contrast too

**Principle:** non-text elements that carry meaning — input borders, focus rings, icon-only controls, chart strokes, toggle states — clear 3:1 against what's adjacent.
**Mechanism:** the boundary of a control *is* the control's signifier. A 1.4:1 hairline border is a design decision that removes the only evidence a text field exists, and it's the most common failure in "clean, minimal" form UI.
**Code signal:**
  - input/select borders at the very light end of the neutral ramp — `border-gray-200` on `bg-white`
  - a focus ring in the accent color on an accent-colored surface, or a ring token the same lightness as the background
  - icon-only buttons whose icon color is a muted text token
**Fix:** step the border/ring/icon token until it clears 3:1 against its adjacent surface — or give the control a filled background instead of relying on the outline.
**Applies when:** cross-lane with `accessibility-review` (WCAG 1.4.11); cite the overlap rather than re-auditing conformance.
**Detect:** STATIC
**Source:** https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html (2023) · https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-appearance.html (2023)

## Color is never the only carrier of meaning

**Principle:** every state, category, or status communicated by color is also communicated by text, icon, shape, or position.
**Mechanism:** hue is the channel most likely to be unavailable — color vision deficiency, grayscale print, a dark theme that shifts the ramp, a screenshot in a bug report. Redundancy costs one icon; its absence costs the meaning entirely.
**Code signal:**
  - a status dot/pill carrying only a background color, no label or icon inside it
  - form validation applied as `border-red-500` with no message, icon, or `aria-invalid`
  - a chart or legend distinguishing series by stroke color alone — no pattern, marker, or direct label
  - "required" or "error" conveyed by a red asterisk with no text equivalent
**Fix:** add the second channel — a label, an icon, a shape, or a direct annotation — keeping color as reinforcement.
**Detect:** STATIC
**Source:** https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html (2023) · https://lawsofux.com/law-of-similarity/ (2020)

## One accent, and semantics built like it

**Principle:** one accent hue owns "this is the action". Success/warning/danger are separate ramps built the same way, not one-off hexes, and none of them is a second accent.
**Mechanism:** an accent works by being the only saturated thing on a neutral field. A second accent halves that signal, and semantic colors used decoratively (a green stat, a red heading) spend a meaning the interface needs later for an actual state.
**Code signal:**
  - two saturated brand hues used interchangeably for interactive elements across a page
  - semantic tokens defined as single values (`--success: #16a34a`) while the brand has a full ramp — no surface/border/text steps to build a banner from
  - success/danger hues used on non-state UI — a green metric, a red section heading
**Fix:** lock one accent; give each semantic color the same step structure as the brand ramp; move decorative uses back to the neutral or accent ramp.
**Applies when:** a second accent is a legitimate deliberate choice in a system that states it — `design-director`'s plan outranks this entry where it does.
**Detect:** STATIC
**Source:** *Refactoring UI* (Wathan & Schoger, 2018) · https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale (2022) — every step has an assigned job, which is what a one-value semantic token lacks

## Accent lands on controls, in states a person caused

**Principle:** the accent appears on **controls**, and on a control only in a state someone caused — hover, focus, selected, active. A resting surface, card, panel, section background, or container never carries it. Three exceptions: the primary action's solid fill, accent ink on text-only controls (links, quiet buttons), and the focus ring. Rest fills come from the neutral ramp, with no accent hue in them.
**Mechanism:** an accent buys exactly one thing — *this responds to you*. Spent on a resting container it's already present before anyone did anything, so it stops distinguishing what will respond from what won't. It also works only by being the sole saturated thing on the field at rest; a tinted panel behind a button is competing with the button for the signal the button needs.
**Code signal:**
  - an accent step as the resting background of a card, panel, banner, section, or page header — `bg-accent-2`, `background: var(--accent-3)` on a container
  - an accent-tinted resting fill on a control, where those steps' actual job is hover/selected/active
  - an `--accent-subtle` / `brand-50` surface token used as a neutral — the tell is a neutral ramp that exists but has no step at that lightness
  - a decorative accent border or left-rule on a non-interactive block
**Fix:** move the rest fill to the neutral ramp's step at the same position and keep the accent steps for the person-caused states; where the emphasis was the point, carry it with the neutral ramp's contrast or with type, not hue.
**Applies when:** the primary action's fill and the focus ring are stated exceptions, not violations. A brand-expressive marketing hero, or a section deliberately flooded with brand color, is direction — `design-director`'s plan outranks this entry where it says so.
**Detect:** STATIC
**Source:** team convention, narrowing two sources that stop short of it — *Refactoring UI* (Wathan & Schoger, 2018), whose palette guidance goes as far as "one, *maybe* two colors that are used for primary actions, emphasizing navigation elements" (https://www.refactoringui.com/previews/building-your-color-palette) without ruling out resting surfaces; and Radix's step model (https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale, 2022), whose accent steps 3–5 stay authored here, for the person-caused states rather than for rest.

## A dark theme is a second scale, not the first one dimmed

**Principle:** dark mode has its own surface and text steps, and its own accent lightness. It is authored, not derived by inverting or reducing opacity.
**Mechanism:** perceived contrast isn't symmetric — light text on dark reads heavier than the reverse (halation), so a face and an accent tuned for a light surface both come out too loud when the surface flips. Inversion also inverts the elevation model: shadow, which is what conveyed depth, is nearly invisible on dark.
**Code signal:**
  - a `.dark` block that only remaps a handful of tokens, leaving the rest resolving to light-theme values
  - dark-theme surfaces produced with `opacity` or `filter: invert()` rather than their own steps
  - the same accent token in both themes (a mid accent that read as vivid on white reads as glaring on near-black)
  - shadow tokens reused unchanged in dark, with no lighter surface step to convey elevation
**Fix:** author dark as a full parallel set of steps — surfaces, text, borders, accent — and express elevation in dark by stepping the surface lighter (see `depth-and-surface`).
**Detect:** STATIC
**Source:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/ (2020) · https://web.dev/articles/color-scheme (2020)
